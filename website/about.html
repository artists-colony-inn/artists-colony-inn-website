Our Story

About the Artists' Colony Inn

Photo — Inn Interior

A boutique guesthouse in the Artists' Quarter

The Artists' Colony Inn sits in the heart of Tzfat's legendary Artists' Quarter — a neighbourhood of ancient stone alleyways, blue-painted doorways, and a creative spirit that has endured for centuries.

The inn occupies a historic building whose walls carry the memory of the city itself. Five individually appointed rooms offer guests a genuine home away from home, with the care and attention that only a small, family-run property can provide.

We believe that where you sleep shapes how you experience a place. At the Artists' Colony Inn, every detail is chosen with that in mind.

Your Hosts

Shlomo & Leah

Shlomo and Leah came to Tzfat by choice — drawn by the city's singular character, its deep spiritual roots, and its living tradition of art and learning. After years of searching for the right place to put down roots in Israel, they found it here.

Running the inn is, for them, an expression of genuine hospitality — the kind rooted in the Jewish value of hachnasat orchim, welcoming guests. Every visitor is received as an honoured guest.

Shlomo brings a lifetime of curiosity — as a musician, a writer, and a student of Kabbalah — to the conversations that happen naturally in a place like this. Leah brings warmth, organisation, and an eye for the details that make a stay truly comfortable.

The City

Tzfat — a city unlike any other

At 900 metres above sea level in the Galilee, Tzfat is Israel's highest city and one of the four holy cities of Judaism. It has been a centre of Kabbalistic learning since the 16th century.

Photo — Tzfat Old City

The old city of Tzfat is compact and walkable, built across a hillside of stone staircases, arched passages, and views over the Galilee that stretch to the Golan Heights on a clear day.

The Artists' Quarter is home to dozens of galleries, studios, and ateliers where painters, sculptors, jewellers, and printmakers work and sell. Ancient synagogues — some dating to the time of Rabbi Joseph Karo and the Ari — are within walking distance of the inn.

Tzfat rewards slow travel. It is a city for walking, sitting, listening, and looking. Guests often say they arrived for a night and stayed for three.
