About the Artists' Colony Inn

The Inn

A boutique inn in the Artists' Quarter of Tzfat's Old City

The Artists' Colony Inn is nestled in the heart of Tzfat's historic Artists' Quarter, a neighborhood of ancient stone alleyways, blue-painted doorways, and a creative spirit that has inspired visitors and residents for centuries.

Housed within a historic building, the inn reflects the character and heritage of the city itself. Its five individually designed suites combine comfort with authenticity, offering guests a true home away from home and the warm, personal hospitality that only a small, family-run property can provide.

We believe that where you stay shapes how you experience a destination. At the Artists' Colony Inn, every detail is thoughtfully chosen to help you connect with the unique atmosphere and timeless charm of Tzfat.

A Building That Remembers

The walls of this building are close to 350 years old, and some of the stones may be older still. A certified local tour guide and retired archaeologist once pointed out that certain stones carry marks belonging to Crusader, Mamluk, and Ottoman times alike, all present in the same walls. Some of those stones may once have been part of the Crusader fortress that stood above Tzfat before it fell to the Mamluks. That fortress is a quiet park today, its own secrets still waiting underground.

Somewhere beneath the building, a buried arch in the basement hints at rooms still hidden below.

This part of the Old City was once home to Tzfat's wealthy Arab families, until the War of Independence in 1948 left the house empty. For a few years it stood quiet. Then, in the early 1950s, the municipality invited artists from the center of the country to settle in the abandoned homes of the Old City, offering them free or heavily subsidized places to live and work. That invitation gave birth to what is known today as the Artists' Quarter, and this building was one of the homes that welcomed them in. One of the paintings in our lobby was made by the artist who once lived and worked within these very walls.

Jeff and Benay Katz later opened the building as a small inn, built around a simple idea: guests should have the privacy of their own room, and the warmth of a shared table when they wanted company. Religious and secular guests could each keep Shabbat their own way, side by side, often becoming friends by the end of a weekend.

Suzan and Danny Zehavi carried that tradition forward for years after them, keeping the inn's doors open to travelers drawn to Tzfat's singular character.

Today, Shlomo and Leah welcome guests in that same spirit, in a building that has already lived several lives, and may still be holding on to a few more.

The Hosts

Shlomo & Leah

Shlomo and Leah chose to make Tzfat their home, drawn by the city's unique character, deep spiritual heritage, and enduring tradition of art and learning.

For them, running the inn is an expression of genuine hospitality, inspired by the Jewish value of hachnasat orchim, welcoming guests into one's home. Every visitor is received with warmth, care, and personal attention.

From Sunday through Friday, guests are offered a chef-prepared breakfast as a complimentary part of their stay. On Shabbat, the inn embraces the peaceful rhythm of the day, maintaining a quiet and restful atmosphere throughout.
