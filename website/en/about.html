About the Artists' Colony Inn

The Inn

A boutique inn in the Artists' Quarter of Tzfat's Old City

Step through our door and you're already somewhere else. Tzfat's Old City, its stone alleyways and blue-painted doorways unchanged in spirit for centuries, opens right into the Artists' Quarter, where the Artists' Colony Inn sits.

The building itself carries that same weight of place. Five individually designed suites sit inside walls that have sheltered families, artists, and travelers long before us, giving guests something a new building simply can't offer: a true sense of having arrived somewhere real.

This inn is built around one idea. The place you stay shapes the whole trip. Every detail here, from the stonework to the welcome, is meant to pull you into Tzfat rather than shelter you from it.

A Building That Remembers

These walls are close to 350 years old, and some of the stones may be older still. A local tour guide, a retired archaeologist, once pointed out that a single wall can carry marks from Crusader, Mamluk, and Ottoman hands alike. Some of those stones may once have stood in the Crusader fortress above Tzfat, before it fell to the Mamluks. That fortress is a quiet park today, its own secrets still waiting underground.

Somewhere beneath our building, a buried arch in the basement hints at rooms still hidden below.

This part of the Old City once belonged to Tzfat's wealthy Arab families, until the War of Independence in 1948 left the house empty. It stood quiet for a few years, until the early 1950s, when the municipality invited artists from the center of the country to settle in the abandoned homes of the Old City, offering them free or subsidized space to live and work. That invitation gave the Artists' Quarter its name, and this building was one of the homes that answered it. One of the paintings in our lobby, hanging where it's always hung, was made by the artist who once lived and worked inside these very walls.

Jeff and Benay Katz later opened the building as a small inn, built around a simple idea: guests should have the privacy of their own room, and the warmth of a shared table when they wanted company. Religious and secular guests could each keep Shabbat their own way, side by side and with mutual respect, often becoming friends by the end of a weekend.

Suzan and Danny Zehavi carried that tradition forward for years after them, keeping the inn's doors open to travelers drawn to Tzfat's singular character.

Today, Shlomo and Leah welcome guests in that same spirit, in a building that has already lived several lives, and may still be holding on to a few more.

The Hosts

Shlomo & Leah

Ours is a Brazil-to-Canada-to-Tzfat story. We were both born and raised in Brazil, built our family together in Canada, and made Aliyah to Israel in 2020. Tzfat wasn't the original plan, we started visiting after our daughter moved here with her family. Like they say, people don't choose Tzfat, Tzfat chooses people, and eventually it chose us too. We settled here ourselves, taking over the inn from the owners before us when they retired.

Running the inn is our way of living out hachnasat orchim, the Jewish value of welcoming guests into one's home. Every visitor gets the same thing: warmth, attention, and care that doesn't feel rehearsed.

From Sunday through Friday, guests are offered a chef-prepared breakfast as a complimentary part of their stay. On Shabbat, the inn embraces the peaceful rhythm of the day, maintaining a quiet and restful atmosphere throughout.

As one guest put it: "We felt right at home. Leah and Shlomo are wonderful hosts, full of information about the city, the customs." — Naomi S.
